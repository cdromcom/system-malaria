## The question

**How can we tell when newly published research has changed what is known about a malaria drug-resistance gene?**

## Why it matters

Malaria parasites evolve, and the genetic mutations that let them survive a drug keep shifting. A new mutation appearing in a new country can mean a drug that worked last year no longer does. The problem is that this news arrives scattered across hundreds of separate papers, so a researcher tracking one gene has no simple way to know when the overall picture has changed enough to act on. This is the challenge question set by Roberto Amato of PATH.

## Where we have got to

> [!info] Current answer
> An alert would need to fire on any of three things: a result that contradicts what earlier studies found, a sharp change in how common a mutation is, or the first sighting of a mutation in a new place or after a long gap.
